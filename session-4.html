Summary

The final session brings the course ideas together. In this session we consolidate learnings around responsibility and ethics with some examples from well conceptualised works. We will also finalise any mini-challenge group work outputs for presentation to the class as well as personal projects at any stage of their development. The last half of the session will be dedicated to presentations, feedback, and Q&A to reflect on learnings and thoughts after engaging with a number of tools and processes.

Recap of Session 4

Read the full Session 4 recap

In Session 4, we went through some live demonstrations with a number of tools to finalise and work through elements of mini-challenges to enhance outputs and wrap up materials for presentations. The tools we explored included Claude, and Canva with and without AI capabilities. Each group presented their solutions for their given mini-challenges and the class was able to query or offer feedback. A few individuals also presented their ideas and works for their personal projects.

One of the examples from the slides includes a service created in Scotland which uses digital systems as well as AI language tools for use in a health and social care setting, designed for workers to aid their processes CoorieWell . The project was conceived in collaboration with staff and teams in the social healthcare environment, then went through a similar iterative process as you have completed in your own group work, refinements, verification, safety and ethics considerations. The early version of the project was then tested in small pilot scale sessions, which then developed into a formalised collaboration with AI ethics experts to evaluate further impacts and considerations Oxford Institute for Ethics in AI . This example is useful since the process includes various levels of responsible actions that can act as an example of a well conceived tool, with end users in mind and clear workflow. A write-up from the founder also reflects on the key areas of ethics which are embedded in the ethos of the project and offers a point for reflection "The Bone Ring": A reflection on responsibility .

The remaining elements of the talk slides touch on economies, both macro for growth in the UK and micro levels which include citizen level economic impacts. Some examples are given for industries which opted for adaptation or overhaul with genAI emergence, and how typical profession grade software or companies producing digital tools may have made their respective choices. A note is made specifically for creative tech spaces, where adaptations produced specific concerns around ethics for artistic productions, as well as future skills gap balanced with the tension of surviving as an industry.

Finally, the final part of the talk slides refer to continued unhelpful language around genAI which produces confusion, and covers some terminology such as 'frontier models'. This topic was followed by commentary on work and intertwined futures around labour, access gaps, and the constrained ownership of current AI services.

Main ideas from the session

  • Applied AI in community & social good contexts requires a duty of care, and iterative processes to design services and tools. Ethical considerations need to be evaluated & formulated in advance with domain experts. Well conceptualised projects can serve well for use cases such as the example provided in class. However, responsibility is shared amongst many people along the way. Primarily, those designing systems and choosing how they will be deployed. In the case of the class example, care was taken to work directly with language models, with layers of work around data storage and access.
  • Responsibility with AI can be embedded in training across disciplines and applied uses, specifically where impact is made clear for any given use. In the case of the example from the blog (The Bone Ring) referring to the Quebec Bridge Engineers, lessons were learned and then implemented into future practice.
  • Economics is a vast field, and reference to economic benefits or growth in the UK can be broken down into macro and micro categories where wider impact and measurements that can track this will be limited to specific industries for macro impacts. In the case of micro level economics, the response of individuals factors differently. On the individual basis economic benefits or fluctuations in day to day finances would be measured and captured on varying grounds, income, spends and so forth, and there is not much evidence here for the impact of AI since most people would be grouped as service users of the main companies providing genAI. However, there are some ideas connecting innovation and impact at these micro levels. Think back to the discussion we had around who can leverage the tools and underlying mechanisms behind AI, and the kind of collaboration and domains where it has shown to be useful (that includes other methods under the umbrella term AI, including machine learning).
  • The nature of labour or work was noted in class discussions, this has been a societal topic of interest for many years and has come to the fore again with wide genAI implementation in the workplace. Laws and regulations will inevitably fluctuate mainly due to the foundations of genAI services being embedded in private companies, as well as how nation states differ in their approaches with data & digital services.
