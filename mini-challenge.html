Mini-Challenge

Mini challenges will be set challenges submitted by community organisations defining a set criteria for you to work through as a group

This part of the class is designed for team collaboration, which means that we will split the the class into roughly 3 groups, with 5 members each. Each group will be given a set challenge to work on. The challenges have been provided by real local community organisations who shared specific tasks that you might work on solving together in your groups, where you can use AI as an assistive tool to help you.

During the mini challenge we will provide you with a detailed description of what the task is for your group, some things you might consider and exactly what we are asking you to. We would like you work in yur groups each session and slowly start preparing an outline or plan for how someone might solve the challenge. In the final session you will submit your process which would look a little like a recipe with instructions. The challenges around digital systems, and we may not expertise or clear understanding around how these work, this is where collaboration, research and optional use of AI chat assistance would offer some further support. to the main task you were dealing with. We will then ask each group to present this to class.

Designing solutions for real world challenges will allow you explore real digital problems at organisational levels, work as a group to break down the problem and collectively consider options. You may use AI tools for guiding what might be needed to solve the digital problem, what the organisation has asked for, and how they operate as well as suggestions for technical possibilities. In addition to this you will consider some important skills like choosing roles in your team & collecting the results in a document or diagram. Finally, sharing your findings in a presentation and providing feedback to others is a great way to start thinking. about what you might prepare, adjust or develop in your own personal projects.

The idea is that if we are to use AI chatbots as part of understanding them, that we might do so in the context of community good uses and create a meaningful context around the task. A note: The organisations who submitted a mini-challenge do not use AI in their current processes for delivering services to their selected community. The problems they have posed are at the operational level for those working at the charity and mainly around using digital services. In some cases the organisation may be focussed on privacy of their participants, such as maintaining details around identities and require solutions which involve both, 1) well applied digital systems as well as 2) preserving privacy in line with UK laws.

Compare two AI chatbot responses

Different AI chatbots may respond differently to the same question. This optional activity lets your group enter one prompt and compare two responses.

Use the activity to examine suggestions, notice differences, and decide what may be useful. You do not need to accept either response, and important information should always be checked independently.

Before you begin

  • Discuss the problem and your own ideas as a group first.
  • Use a general, fictional, or course-related prompt.
  • Do not enter names, addresses, contact details, passwords, private documents, or sensitive personal information.
  • Ask the chatbots for suggestions, comparisons, or explanations rather than asking them to produce the whole solution.
  • Review both responses together before recording any ideas.

If the embedded activity is difficult to use, you can open it in a separate browser tab.

A few things to note from this large language model comparison. This tool puts two well known AI chatbots side by side. One is from OpenAI (ChatGPT) and the other from Anthropic (Claude). Note the name next to the models, for example -> Sonnet 4.5. The model names will keep changing over time, as the companies improve and build upon them. Next note the prompt box for you to enter text. In this site, we are using the chatbots without visiting the official websites of the tools.

Discuss the responses

  • What ideas appear in both responses?
  • What is different between them?
  • Does either response misunderstand the challenge or audience?
  • Are the suggestions affordable, accessible, and realistic?
  • What information would need to be checked elsewhere?
  • Which suggestions, if any, could support your group’s own plan?
