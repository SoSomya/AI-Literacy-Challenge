Further Resources

This page brings together free resources for anyone who would like to keep learning after the AI Literacy Challenge sessions. These links are optional and are here to support practice, confidence-building, and further exploration.

Some resources are short videos, some are practical exercises, and some are useful explainers. You can choose what feels most useful for your own learning. These resources have been collected from numerous places (you can find out if you read "Source" and follow the links on the buttons). The current list is a small set (there are so many resources out there)! We can can keep adding to this list, but to start with this is a good amount.

Free short courses and videos

Use this section for free video courses, short tutorials, public lectures, and beginner-friendly explainers.

AI for Education — Unit 1

A free introductory course from Khan Academy and Code.org. Unit 1 is a good starting point for learning about generative AI, large language models, and how AI tools are changing education.

How to use: Start with Unit 1. Watch a short section, then note one thing you learned and one question you still have.

Level: Introductory
Format: Video / short course
Credit: Khan Academy and Code.org
Tags: AI basics, generative AI, education

Living with AI

A free, beginner-friendly online course about what AI is, how it is shaping daily life, and how people can think critically about its opportunities, risks, ethics, and social impact.

How to use: Good for revisiting the basics after Session 1 or Session 2. No coding or technical background is needed.

Level: Introductory
Format: Free online course with signup
Credit: Scottish AI Alliance / Living with AI
Tags: AI basics, ethics, society, Scotland

Exercises and practice tasks

Use this section for activities people can try independently, such as prompt practice, simple design tasks, digital confidence exercises, or AI checking activities.

Compare Large Language Models

This AI Pedagogy Project activity helps you compare responses from different large language models. It is useful for noticing that different AI tools can answer the same question in different ways.

How to use: Try the same question in more than one model. Compare which answer is clearer, more useful, more careful, or easier to understand.

Level: Intermediate
Format: Exercise / practical comparison task
Credit: AI Pedagogy Project
Tags: prompts, comparison, LLMs, checking outputs

Open resource

Configure Large Language Models

This AI Pedagogy Project guide introduces settings that can shape AI outputs, including system prompts, temperature, and maximum tokens. It is useful for understanding why AI answers can vary.

How to use: Use this after you are comfortable with basic prompting. Notice how small setting changes can affect the style, length, or predictability of a response.

Level: Intermediate
Format: Exercise / guide
Credit: AI Pedagogy Project
Tags: LLM settings, prompts, temperature

Open resource

AI Quests

A free game-based learning experience from Google Research and Stanford Accelerator for Learning. The quests use real-world problems to show how data, models, and human decisions shape AI outputs.

How to use: Try one quest and think about where human judgement appears in the process. What data is used? What choices are made? What could go wrong?

Level: Introductory
Format: Game / interactive learning activity
Credit: Google Research and Stanford Accelerator for Learning
Tags: data, models, responsible AI, game

Open resource

Mix & Move from Code.AI

A free music mixing game, which walks you through how prompts, AI generation and your own actions work together to make a small music mix using built in instrumental audio files.

How to use: Walk through the instructions and read the pop out information as you work through to the final mix you make. You will need to login with an account to use for free. Sign up as a student/learner.

Level: Introductory
Format: Game / interactive learning activity
Source:Code.AI
Tags: data, models, responsible AI, game

AI Confidential with Hannah Fry

A BBC documentary series presented by Professor Hannah Fry. The series explores real human stories connected to AI, including both the harm AI can cause and the potential it may hold for society.

How to use: Watch one episode and note one example of AI benefit, one example of AI harm, and one question you would ask before using a similar tool.

Level: Introductory to intermediate
Format: Documentary series
Credit: BBC and Hannah Fry
Tags: AI in society, ethics, real-world impact

Format: Companion activities / videos
Credit: The Open University and BBC

Apps and examples

This section will link apps or other tools which use computational and have been used for community projects.

Merlin Bird ID

A free app from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. Merlin can help identify birds from sounds, photos, or simple questions about what you saw. This app uses machine learning and has helped to note important information about various birds in different parts of the world.

How to use: Try the app outdoors and compare the suggestion with what you can observe. Treat the result as a helpful clue, not a guaranteed answer.

Project idea: This could support a local nature walk, community birdwatching activity, environmental awareness project, or neighbourhood map.

Level: Introductory
Format: Mobile app
Credit: Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Cornell University
Tags: nature, environment, sound ID, citizen science
