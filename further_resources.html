Further Resources

This page brings together free resources for anyone who would like to keep learning after the AI Literacy Challenge sessions. These links are optional and are here to support practice, confidence-building, and further exploration.

Some resources are short videos, some are practical exercises, and some are useful explainers. You can choose what feels most useful for your own learning. These resources have been collected from numerous places (you can check 'credit' for the source of the linked item and click the button to learn more). The current list is a small set. We can can keep adding to this list as we go along, but the below items are a good starting point.

Explore how AI works

This section is for activities you can try independently, such as prompt practice, simple design tasks, digital confidence exercises, or AI checking activities.

Interactive activity

Compare Large Language Models

This AI Pedagogy Project activity helps you compare responses from different large language models. It is useful for noticing that different AI tools can answer the same question in various ways.

How to use: Try the same question in more than one model. Compare which answer is clearer, more useful, more careful, or easier to understand.

Level: Intermediate
Format: Exercise / practical comparison task
Credit: AI Pedagogy Project
Tags: prompts, comparison, LLMs, checking outputs

Interactive guide

Configure Large Language Models

This AI Pedagogy Project guide introduces settings that can shape AI outputs, including system prompts, temperature, and maximum tokens. It is useful for understanding why AI answers can vary.

How to use: Use this after you are comfortable with basic prompting and after week 2 of the course. Notice how small setting changes can affect the style, length, or predictability of a response.

Level: Intermediate
Format: Exercise / guide
Credit: AI Pedagogy Project
Tags: LLM settings, prompts, temperature

Try an activity

Use this section for activities people can try independently, such as prompt practice, simple design tasks, digital confidence exercises, or AI checking activities.

Interactive learning activity

AI Quests

A free game-based learning experience from Google Research and Stanford Accelerator for Learning. The quests use real-world problems to show how data, models, and human decisions shape AI outputs.

How to use: Try one quest and think about where human judgement appears in the process. The examples used here are around applications of AI (the numerous methods that fall under this term). Many of the examples are from large team projects which included many experts. One of the examples is the flood forecasting project. The main thing to note is that complex projects like the flood forecasting example were carefully curated and not the same as everyday AI use that we might employ.

Level: Introductory
Format: Game / interactive learning activity
Credit: Google Research and Stanford Accelerator for Learning
Tags: data, models, responsible AI, game

Game and interactive activity

Mix & Move from Code.AI

A free music mixing game, which walks you through how prompts, AI generation and your own actions work together to make a small music mix using built in instrumental audio files.

How to use: Walk through the instructions and read the pop out information as you work through to the final mix you make. You will need to login with an account to use for free. Sign up as a student/learner.

Level: Introductory
Format: Game / interactive learning activity
Source: Code.AI
Tags: data, models, responsible AI, game

AI, people and society

Use this section for accessible articles, podcasts, interviews, animations, or public explainers about AI, digital technology, and society.

Documentary series

AI Confidential with Professor Hannah Fry

A BBC documentary series presented by Professor Hannah Fry. The series explores real human stories connected to AI, including both the harm AI can cause and the potential it may hold for society.

How to use: Watch one episode and note one example of AI benefit, one example of AI harm, and one question you would ask before using a similar tool.

Level: Introductory to intermediate
Format: Documentary series
Credit: BBC and Hannah Fry
Tags: AI in society, ethics, real-world impact

AI Decoded

A BBC News programme exploring current developments, questions, and debates around artificial intelligence. Different episodes look at how AI may affect technology, work, public life, relationships, creativity, and society.

Community projects and useful examples

This section notes some apps or other tools which use computational methods like machine learning and have been used for community projects or social good.

Mobile app

Merlin Bird ID

A free app from the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. Merlin can help identify birds from sounds, photos, or simple questions about what you saw. This app uses machine learning and has helped to note important information about various birds in different parts of the world based on audio.

How to use: Try the app outdoors and compare the suggestion with what you can observe. Based on class teaching, is it clear how the sounds are categorised/classified into possible bird species?

Level: Introductory
Format: Mobile app
Credit: Cornell Lab of Ornithology, Cornell University
Tags: nature, environment, sound ID, citizen science
