Personal Project Build

Project outline across sessions

In the project section of class we will use the 5-sheet paper-based design tool to help trace and track ideas as well as adaptations you might think of as you go along this format will be described to you further in class to help you use it effectively.

The worksheets used in this part of class adapt the Five Design-Sheet method developed by Jonathan C. Roberts and later expanded with Christopher Headleand and Panagiotis D. Ritsos. The original method supports low-fidelity sketching, divergent thinking, development of alternative designs, and convergence toward a final realisation. This version adapts the method for AI-supported community project design, using PESTLE analysis and the UK government’s AI regulatory principles as reflection prompts.

  • Session 1: Ideation

    Choose a real-world need, audience, and possible project direction.

    Sheet 1: The real-world need. Who is this for? What problem or opportunity are we focusing on?

  • Session 2: Combine and refine

    Narrow the idea, decide how the project might look, and choose a likely format.

    Sheet 2: What people need to do? What does the person/group need to know, decide, make, find, compare, remember, or share?

    Sheet 3: The proposed solution. What are we designing? A guide, workflow, website, template, poster, video, or other support?

  • Session 3: Initial design and build protocol

    Create the first version, workflow, draft, prototype, or implementation plan.

    Sheet 3 continued: The proposed solution. What are we designing? A guide, workflow, website, template, poster, video, or other support?

    Sheet 4: Tools, risks, and support. Which tools could help? What should not go into AI if AI is part of the project? What privacy, bias, access, or confidence issues matter?

  • Session 4: Final project build and share

    Wrap-up the first version of the project. It does not need to be polished. Prepare a short explanation and identify next steps.

    Sheet 5: Final project snapshot. What is the idea, who does it help, how would it work, and what would the next step be?

Personal project tracks

These tracks offer starting points for your personal project. You can choose one, adapt one, or combine ideas across tracks.

Track 1: Social engagement in the community

  • Game jams in your neighbourhood.
  • Dungeons and Dragons group: creating narrative, characters, music and visuals.
  • Local sports group: setting up a community group and schedule.
  • Music days and organising arts events: designing materials and sharing.

Track 2: Environmental

  • Highlighting loved spaces: making a small tool for visiting spaces.
  • Local clean-up and group activities: sharing an initiative and setting up a group.
  • Designing spaces for your local community: responding to calls from local authorities or developing a proposal.

Track 3: Advocacy and community governance

  • Fundraising for a local project with a local organisation.
  • Developing a proposal for a local authority in your neighbourhood.
  • Responding to letters or writing letters.
  • Creating a digital notice board or newsletter.

Track 4: Mutual exchange

  • Setting up a way to share upcoming events, projects, or materials between local organisations.
  • Neighbours and street community care.

Track 5: Personal and community development

  • Making a website to showcase work in your locality and highlight organisations.
  • Making a workflow for yourself and others to manage time.
  • Navigating job applications, CVs and cover letters, helping yourself and others by creating a resource.

Choosing a tool for your project

The best tool depends on what you are trying to make. Please share your ideas with us in class and we can discuss how to approach the project and which tools can best suit your project needs.

Your project idea might have a few steps, for instance creating a series of park clean-up sessions and picnic for your local community. The steps for this project might include, making a poster, having a set timetable, coordinating sign-ups and so forth. There are numerous existing software and tools you might use for each of these steps, such as creating the posters or coordinating sign ups. Depending on your experience with the skills required along the project, you may need to conduct some research or require assistance with tasks such as clear language and font design, or making a privacy preserving sign up form.

There are digital tools that exist for these tasks. You can leverage AI assistance to refine, or further develop how you might implement the idea in some parts of the project. AI chat formats can support further understanding and development of some of the skills required if you ask for signposting to other resources. You may start working with a graphics building app to make some elements for your poster, and require support in understanding the documentation for all of the functions and buttons in the software.

Thereby, two ways to use the AI chatbots during your project build would be 1) for signposting and suggestions on how to implement, suited to your needs. 2) to use a learning guide as you navigate new software or approaches for your projects. This does mean it is a foolproof approach, you will still be the lead on your project, you will have to develop the base idea, make the relevant ethical considerations, and evaluate what you need before liaising with an AI system.

Taking the lessons learned from the mini challenges and lectures on the course can help guide whether or how you might use AI assistance. This is not mandatory or prescriptive, simply a few ways to think about the uses that you can decide for yourself.

Things to consider

  • How accessible is the project for the age groups who would use it?
  • Are there any concerns or ethical issues with the format?
  • Are the selected tools suitable for continued development and iteration?
