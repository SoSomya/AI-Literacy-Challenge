Summary

In this first session, we will explore what artificial intelligence means. We will look at common words such as AI, data, computation, automation, machine learning and generative AI, and connect them to everyday examples such as search tools, recommendations, voice assistants and image or text generation.

The session will also introduce course goals, the group mini challenge and personal project work.

Session 1 Q&A

Do I need to know about AI already?

No. The session starts from the beginning. You can ask questions at any point.

What should I bring?

We will be liaising with you on devices to use in class, you do not have to worry about bringing anything.

What is the mini challenge?

The mini challenge is a group design activity. Your group will use AI and other tools to plan a practical solution to a real-world challenge.

What is the personal project?

The personal project is your own idea. You will work on developing the idea, and the teaching team can help you with understanding which tools would best support you, both AI and non AI tools.

Does my project need to be finished today?

No. In Session 1, the aim is to begin with sketching out the main ideas. You are choosing a possible direction, and working on it weekly using guided handouts and in-class support.

Recap of Session 1

In Session 1, we began by exploring what the term artificial intelligence can include. AI is not one single method, it is an umbrella term to capture many types of methods and tools. The wider field includes machine learning, generative AI, agent-based systems, and fixed rule-based systems. We also discussed the roles of data, information, computation, programming a computer, and computer systems.

We considered how ideas from subjects including mathematics, computer science, biology (neuroscience), and philosophy have contributed to the development of AI. We also looked at how earlier technologies and ideas about machines have influenced the systems and tools we use today.

We explored how machines work with images, sounds, words, and other forms of information. Machine-learning systems can use examples and data to identify patterns and make predictions. Generative AI tools can produce text, images, sound, code, or other content in response to instructions from us.

We introduced large language models and AI chatbots (which use language based prompts). These tools can produce fluent, human-like responses, but this does not mean that they understand, remember, imagine, or experience the world in the same way as a person. Their responses still need human judgement and careful checking.

Main ideas from the session

  • AI is a broad term covering different methods and systems.
  • Not every automated or computer-based system uses AI.
  • Data and information play an important role in machine learning.
  • Machine-learning systems can identify patterns and make predictions from examples.
  • Generative AI can produce new content in response to a prompt.
  • A prompt is the instruction, question, or request given to an AI tool.
  • Human-like chatbot responses do not prove human-like understanding.
  • People remain responsible for reviewing and checking AI-assisted work.

Check your understanding

Use these short questions to revisit some of the main ideas from Session 1. This is a practice activity rather than a test.

Choose an answer to receive immediate feedback. Your answers are not stored or submitted.

1. What does artificial intelligence describe in this course?

Correct

Artificial intelligence is a broad field rather than one single tool or method. It includes different approaches such as machine learning, generative AI, agent-based systems and some rule-based systems.

Not quite

The correct answer is a broad field containing different methods and systems . A chatbot is one possible AI application, but it does not represent the whole field.

2. Which example best describes generative AI?

Correct

Generative AI can produce new text, images, audio, video, code or other content in response to a prompt.

Not quite

The correct answer is a tool producing an image, sound, video or text from a written prompt . The other examples follow fixed instructions, calculations or rules.

3. How can a machine-learning system learn to perform a task?

Correct

Machine-learning systems use examples and data to identify patterns. These patterns can then support predictions, classifications or other outputs.

Not quite

The correct answer is by using examples and data to identify patterns . This does not give the computer human experiences, feelings or understanding.

4. What is a prompt?

Correct

A prompt is an instruction, question, request or other input given to an AI tool.

Not quite

The correct answer is an instruction, question or request given to an AI tool . The prompt is the input rather than the response produced by the tool.

5. An AI chatbot produces fluent, human-like writing. What can we conclude?

Correct

An AI chatbot can produce convincing language without understanding or experiencing the world in the same way as a person. Its outputs still require human judgement and checking.

Not quite

The correct answer is fluent writing does not prove human-like understanding . A response may sound confident or natural while still being incomplete, misleading or wrong.

AI

Click to reveal definition.

Artificial Intelligence

Computer systems programmed by experts that can produce predictions, support decisions, make recommendations, or generate content. The listed tasks would typically require human intelligence, therefore when machines produce these outputs it is known as artificial intelligence. The term AI is one umbrella word which covers a few different categories of computer science & data science (machine learning, neural networks, & deep learning). Whilst these methods are different in size and scope, they rely on initial data inputs, followed by mathematical functions, discovering patterns in the data and in some cases 'training' the model to 'learn', in order to produce outputs. These methods were developed by various researchers, largely for analysing data, making sense of complex systems, predictions based on existing data and classification. Typically, a set task is given for designing a machine-based program, for example weather forecasting would include the task of predicting weather based on prior data. However, the range of tasks under the AI umbrella is quite wide and depends on which exact method is used, for which task, and how data was utilised in a given model.

Source: Class tutor

Information

Data that has been given meaning or context so that people or systems can understand and use it. When data are put together, have narrative around how they fit, they can be given context which can inform us and let us know what it represents as information about that context. Example: in this class you have been given a lot of information about AI, this is composed of teaching, activities, insights from each other, insights from the tutor and different types of resources.

Computation

Performing calculations or steps to calculate information, individually or via a computer system.

Algorithm

Algorithm

A set of instructions or steps that a computer follows to complete a task or solve a problem. The series of steps might include calculations in a specific order, collating data, adjusting values, and guiding a process. It may follow the pattern of Input (data provides the examples) -> Calculations (mathematics to calculate) -> Steps (algorithm for organising steps) -> Outputs (The computer or model produces the output).

Source: Class tutor

Automation

Click to reveal definition.

Automation

When a machine or software carries out a task with little or no repeated human effort. You can think of automation as deriving from the word automatic. Typically, an automatic process would be pre-set in a specific way so that a process can run again and again without too much handling. A simple example would be setting up a news feed widget on your computer that is set to pull in news items from your selected themes or topics weekly. Another example from manufacturing in factories could be a set process for a product on a conveyor belt where the machinery is programmed to place the caramel topping on every ice cream cone which passes through in the sequence.

Source: Class tutor.

Machine Learning

Click to reveal definition.

Machine Learning

A type of AI where a computer system uses data to find patterns and make predictions or groupings (classification). Typically, the patterns that exist in data are 'learned' by the machine learning model, therefore human instruction in the pattern discovery is not required. However, humans guide and setup the data in different ways at the start, they decide what task the machine learning model should perform, and evaluate its performance based on specific goals. Machine learning is composed of a number of methods and approaches, with initial foundations in statistics and building up from there. One example where machine learning has been implemented and is noted as a classic case, is the process by which spam emails are filtered in your mailbox. Distinguishing spam from not spam would be called classification. The model requires lots of data (examples of emails), from which the patterns in spam versus not spam are learned from exposure, then the classification is made possible. Sometimes an important email might end up in spam, but in more cases than not, the program does well to filter out spam emails so they never make it to your inbox folder. Other uses of machine learning expand into research with data from health, environment and so forth. Academic researchers as well as private companies might use these methods to optimise a tool, analysis or product.

Source: Class tutor (reference to spam emails, task, performance and learned experience derived from Tom Mitchell)

Generative AI

Generative AI

Generative AI is a subset of AI capable of generating text, images, video or other forms of output by using probabilistic models trained across one or more domains. These models learn from large amounts of specially-curated training data to discern and replicate complex patterns and structures. The output generated by these models mimics the characteristics learned from that training data, enabling many novel applications – ranging from personalised content generation, to advanced analysis and evaluation, and aiding creative processes.

Source: UK Gov
