Setup Guides & Tools

This page introduces the some of the tools we may use during the AI Literacy Challenge. Some tools will be used in class, while others are optional suggestions for personal project work. You do not need to try every tool. As we begin with the course you will develop your own ideas and we can best direct which types of tools would suit for your ideas, these are just a couple to give you an idea.

We will be using free plans on the tools we explore, and you will not be expected to make any payments or submit any payment information on any site.

Before using any tool, think carefully about privacy, access, confidence, and whether the tool is genuinely useful for your task. Do not upload private, sensitive, or identifying information into AI tools. If in doubt, check in with the teaching team.

Before class

The most useful thing to have ready is an email address you can access, with a password you remember. Many tools will require you to sign in before you can save work or use them fully.

  • Ask for help if a sign-in screen, password, or verification code appears.
  • Only use accounts and tools you feel comfortable using.
  • Do not share passwords with anyone.

Tools we might explore use in class

These tools may support live activities, group work, shared notes, or access to participant materials.

Obsidian Notes

Obsidian notes app on the computer/laptop.

Used for:Making notes, saving notes, links, and connecting ideas with a visual diagram
Account needed?No

Obsidian Notes will be installed on the laptops we will use already. This is a simple note taking application and can be be a useful way to store notes, thoughts, plans digitally on a computer.

Mentimeter

Mentimeter may be used for live polls, questions, quizzes, or quick check-ins during the session. You usually join by going to menti.com and entering the code shown by the tutor.

Used for: live questions, polls, confidence checks
Account needed? Not for participants joining a poll

Open Mentimeter

Padlet

Padlet may be used as a shared board for ideas, questions, reflections, or group notes. Please use respectful comments, emojis, images, and GIFs.

Used for: shared ideas, group reflections, class notes
Account needed? The tutor will explain this in class

Open Padlet

Course materials folder

Participant worksheets and class materials will be shared through a controlled-access folder. This means access can be managed for registered participants.

Used for: worksheets, handouts, support materials

AI tools

These tools are examples of AI assistants or AI-supported tools. We do not endorse any one tool. These are examples of possible generative AI chat based tools we may engage with depending on the tasks at hand. You may not wish to directly use the tools, and this is okay. Activities for this programme are designed to engage your design thinking, problem solving, and critical evaluation. We still encourage you to participate in the group and individual activities in ways that work best for you.

Claude

An AI assistant that can help with writing, reasoning, summarising, editing, and working through longer ideas or documents. We are not selecting this tool for any specific or personal reasons. We specify a singular tool since it is nice to work with one tool across the class and this one is familiar.

Could help with: reflective writing, project explanations, structuring a plan

Open Claude

Gemini

Google’s AI assistant. It may be useful for asking questions, since it works alongside existing Google tools.

Could help with: suggesting digital tools to explore, guidance on how to use a particular software, and answering questions.

Open Gemini

Canva

A design tool with AI-supported features. It may help people make posters, simple slides, social media images, event materials, or visual project drafts.

Could help with: posters, flyers, campaign materials, presentations, visual plans

Open Canva AI

Responsible use reminders

  • Do not enter private personal details into AI tools.
  • Do not upload someone else’s image, writing, or information without permission.
  • Check important information against trusted sources.
  • Use AI as support, not as the final decision-maker.
  • Ask for help if you are unsure whether a tool is safe or suitable.
